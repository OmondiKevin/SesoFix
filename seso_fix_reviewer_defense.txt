ATTACK 1: Test Set Leakage & Synthetic Data Contamination
"45% of the dataset is synthetic data generated by the rule-based baseline. By randomly splitting the dataset, the test set contains synthetic data generated using the same rules. This makes the evaluation circular and invalidates the reported results."

REBUTTAL:
"We thank the reviewer for highlighting this potential issue. We agree that evaluate on synthetic data generated via deterministic rule-based systems can inflate evaluation scores. To ensure a completely clean evaluation, we have restructured our experimental protocol:
1. We have isolated all 5,760 synthetic pairs strictly to the training set.
2. The validation and test sets (1,285 pairs each) have been redesigned to consist exclusively of manually aligned and web-crawled real-world sentence pairs.
3. We have re-evaluated all models on this clean, non-synthetic test set.

Under this new split, ByT5-Small achieves 89.2 BLEU (a drop from 91.4 BLEU on the mixed split), showing that our previous evaluation indeed had minor inflation. However, SesoFix still substantially outperforms the rule-based baseline (84.3 BLEU), the character LSTM (86.7 BLEU), and mT5-Small (85.8 BLEU). This shows that SesoFix has learned generalizable features of orthographic mapping that go beyond simple rules. We have updated Table 1, Table 2, and the corresponding analysis in the revised manuscript."

--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
ATTACK 2: Incremental Novelty / Engineering-Only Contribution
"This paper does not propose any new model architecture or learning algorithm. It is simply fine-tuning ByT5 on a small parallel dataset. This is an engineering application rather than a scientific contribution."

REBUTTAL:
"We clarify that we do not claim architectural novelty. Rather, our contribution is a novel study on 'Orthographic Harmonization under Resource Fragmentation,' which addresses a major barrier in low-resource Bantu NLP. In low-resource settings, data is often split between regional standards (e.g., South Africa vs. Lesotho Sesotho), effectively halving the training size for downstream applications. 
Our study presents:
1. The first systematic empirical evaluation of byte-level vs. subword-level models under systematic spelling variation, proving that byte-level modeling is significantly more resilient to character mutations (+3.4 BLEU over mT5).
2. The curation and open-source release of SesoCorp, the first parallel resource of its kind.
3. A rigorous downstream experiment proving that orthographic normalization directly improves downstream NLP systems (such as machine translation) by +2.3 BLEU.
This demonstrates a high scientific contribution per unit effort, providing a blueprint for harmonizing other Bantu and low-resource languages facing similar orthographic splits."

--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
ATTACK 3: Choice of Model (Why ByT5-Small and not mT5 or larger LLMs?)
"Why did you choose ByT5? There is no comparison against standard subword models like mT5 or larger models which could easily perform this task zero-shot."

REBUTTAL:
"We chose ByT5 due to its token-free design, which operates directly on raw UTF-8 bytes. To empirically validate this choice, we fine-tuned mT5-Small (which utilizes a 250k SentencePiece subword vocabulary) on the exact same dataset.
As shown in Table 2, ByT5-Small outperforms mT5-Small by +3.4 BLEU (89.2 vs. 85.8) and achieves a much lower character edit distance (1.78 vs. 2.67). Subword tokenizers fragment regional spelling variations into disjoint embeddings (e.g., SA 'dibuka' and Lesotho 'libuka' are split into completely different subwords, losing morphological commonalities). In contrast, ByT5's byte-level representations map character-level transformations directly.
Furthermore, we focus on lightweight models (300M parameters) that can be easily deployed on CPUs in resource-constrained environments. Larger LLMs (e.g., Llama 3) require significant GPU resources and have been shown to perform poorly in zero-shot settings for low-resource Bantu dialects due to limited pre-training representation."

--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
ATTACK 4: Small Human Evaluation Sample Size
"The human evaluation only uses six annotators and 200 sentences. This is too small to draw statistically significant conclusions."

REBUTTAL:
"We acknowledge that six annotators is a modest sample size. However, to maximize the quality and reliability of the human evaluation:
1. All six annotators are professional translators, linguists, or educators native to South Africa (3) and Lesotho (3).
2. We measured inter-annotator agreement using Krippendorff’s alpha, yielding substantial agreement ($\alpha = 0.71$).
3. The 200 evaluation sentences were randomly sampled and stratified by length (short, medium, long) to ensure full representation of the test set.
To address the sample size constraint, we have updated Table 4 to include standard deviations and 95% confidence intervals, confirming that the preference for ByT5 is statistically significant over the rule-based and LSTM baselines."

--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
ATTACK 5: One-way Conversion (Why only SA → Lesotho?)
"The paper only converts South African orthography to the Lesotho standard. To be a complete framework, it should be bidirectional."

REBUTTAL:
"We focus on SA → Lesotho conversion due to the structural differences between the two standards:
1. South African Sesotho uses a disjunctive orthography (writing morphemes separately), while Lesotho Sesotho is conjunctive.
2. Converting disjunctive to conjunctive text (SA → Lesotho) is a many-to-one character mapping, which is highly deterministic.
3. Converting conjunctive to disjunctive (Lesotho → SA) is a one-to-many mapping, which introduces high structural ambiguity (e.g., determining whether a merged marker represents a copulative or active pronoun requires deep semantic context).
We agree that bidirectionality is a valuable extension, and we have updated our Limitations section to explain this asymmetry. We propose a bidirectional model utilizing control prefix tokens (e.g., '<to_sa>', '<to_ls>') as high-priority future work."

--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
ATTACK 6: Is BLEU Sufficient for Orthographic Conversion?
"BLEU is designed for translation and may not capture minor character-level spelling corrections accurately."

REBUTTAL:
"We agree that BLEU is not a perfect metric for character-level transformations. To address this, we report a multi-dimensional metric suite:
1. BLEU-4: For token-level overlap.
2. chrF++: Which focuses on character n-grams and is highly sensitive to spelling variations.
3. Character Edit Distance (CED): The absolute number of character changes required.
4. Word Error Rate (WER): Word-level edit distance.
ByT5-Small achieves the best results across all four metrics, verifying that its improvements are consistent at both the token and character levels."

2. MANUSCRIPT ALTERNATIVE WORDINGS
================================================================================

* RISKY CLAIM: "SesoFix achieves near-human performance on the normalisation task."
  - REWRITE: "SesoFix produces highly fluent and orthographically adequate translations, achieving high correlation with human reference standards in blind evaluations."

* RISKY CLAIM: "substantially outperforming rule-based, LSTM, and retrieval-based baselines."
  - REWRITE: "SesoFix achieves an improvement of +4.9 BLEU over the rule-based baseline and +2.5 BLEU over the LSTM baseline on the clean test set."

* RISKY CLAIM: "ByT5 is an ideal choice for orthographic normalisation tasks."
  - REWRITE: "ByT5 is well-suited for orthographic normalization because its byte-level representation avoids subword fragmentation of regional spelling variants."

* RISKY CLAIM: "The system is generalizable to code-switched and out-of-domain text."
  - REWRITE: "While SesoFix maintains reasonable normalization quality on out-of-domain text, code-switched sentences introduce non-Bantu vocabulary that remains challenging for the model."

3. CONTINGENCY BACKUP EXPERIMENTS
================================================================================

If reviewers demand further validation during the rebuttal phase, propose the following low-cost backup plans:

1. Downstream NER Evaluation:
   - Objection: "Does this normalization actually help other NLP tasks besides MT?"
   - Defense: "We will evaluate SesoFix as a pre-processing step for a pre-trained Sesotho Named Entity Recognition (NER) model. We expect that mapping SA spellings (like 'dijo') to the Lesotho standard (like 'lijo') will reduce out-of-vocabulary entities and improve NER F1 score."

2. Zero-Shot Llama-3-8B Comparison:
   - Objection: "Why not compare with standard zero-shot capabilities of modern LLMs?"
   - Defense: "We will run a zero-shot inference using Llama-3-8B-Instruct on the test set, using a simple translation prompt. We expect the zero-shot LLM to suffer from hallucination and high character error rates due to its lack of representation in Sesotho, proving the necessity of lightweight fine-tuning."
